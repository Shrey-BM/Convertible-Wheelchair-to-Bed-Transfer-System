# Subsystem 5-Docking and Safety

## Safety Mechanisms

Every safety feature on this chair is purely mechanical. There are no sensors, batteries or electronics that can fail. Each lock is a part the caregiver can see and feel working.

## 1. Wheel brakes (Caster brakes)
All four wheels have a foot pedal. One press locks the wheel so it can't roll, and locks the swivel so it can't turn. The brakes stay on whenever the chair is parked, being raised or lowered, reclined, or docked to the bed. They come off only while the chair is being pushed from place to place.

## 2. Bed clamp (Dock clamp)
Before any transfer, the chair is clamped to the bed frame on both sides, like a hand gripping the edge of a table.
- A swing arm reaches out to the bed frame (works with a gap of up to 8 cm between chair and frame).
- Two jaws pin in place above and below the frame edge, in 15 mm steps.
- Two hand screws close the last few millimetres.

Only about 44 kg of holding force per side is needed to stop the chair tipping or sliding away during a transfer. A hand-tightened screw gives roughly 340 kg, nearly 8 times more.
It fits mattresses 9.5–28 cm thick and bed frames 2–10.5 cm deep (mattress + frame up to 32 cm).

## 3. Transit locks
Each sliding board (cassette) is pinned to the chair by a spring-loaded steel pin that goes across the direction of sliding, so the board can't creep out while the chair is being pushed around.
- The pin is locked by default. To unlock it, the caregiver pulls the knob and gives it a quarter turn so it stays out.
- When the board slides back home, it stops against a lip on the post (part 69), and the pin snaps back in by itself.

## 4. Backrest locks: two independent locks
The backrest can only move when the caregiver does two separate things on purpose, so it can't drop by accident.
- Gas spring lock: The gas spring is locked except while the caregiver squeezes the release lever under the head handle. Letting it go locks it again instantly.
- Recline detent pin: A red spring-loaded pin clicks into a hole when the backrest is fully upright (80°) or fully flat (0°). It must be pulled out before the backrest can move.

## 5. Flat-position hard stop (part 65)
When the chair is flat, two small aluminium blocks at the ends of the backrest frame rest solidly on the seat frame and carry the patient's weight. This keeps the load off the locking pin and makes sure the bed surface is exactly flat, every time.

## 6. Self-locking lead screw
The chair is raised and lowered with a hand crank that turns a screw. The screw thread is shallow enough that the patient's weight can never turn it backwards. Stopping the cranking keeps the chair stays exactly where it is. No extra brake is needed.

## 7. Side rails during transfer (Armrests 46 + transfer bar 44)
- Only the bed-side armrest is removed for a transfer. The caregiver-side armrest stays on as a side rail.
- The transfer bar, used to push the cassettes, also acts as a rail on the caregiver side, so the patient can't roll off that way.

## 8. No rubbing or dragging on the skin (CONVEY / FREE modes, T-handle)
- CONVEY (handle in): The belt carries the patient across. The patient lies still on the belt and is never dragged.
- FREE (handle out): The belt rolls freely, so the boards can slide in or out from under the patient without rubbing the skin or pulling the sheet.

The boards are only 2.8 cm thick and glide 3 mm above the mattress, so they never dig into the bed.

## 9. Stable base
With a 120 kg patient, the chair only tips on a slope steeper than about 20° (worst case, at full height). A wheelchair ramp is only about 5°, so the margin is about 4 times. Even if someone leans on the head end with the force of about 25 kg, it stays put.

## 10. Built-in strength margin
The chair is rated for a 120 kg patient, and every part is designed for 1.5 times that load (180 kg). The lifting arms are about 4 times stronger than they need to be.

## Transfer checklist (in order)
1. Park next to the bed and press all 4 wheel pedals.
2. Crank the chair to mattress height. The screw holds it there.
3. Recline to flat. The red detent pin must click into the flat (0°) hole.
4. Swing the clamp arms to the bed frame, pin the jaws above and below it, and tighten both screws.
5. Lift out the bed-side armrest. The caregiver-side armrest stays on as a rail.
6. Pull both transit pins (pull + quarter turn), fit the transfer bar, choose CONVEY or FREE, and transfer.
7. Slide the boards back home, turn the transit pins back so they snap in, undock, refit the armrest, and set both handles to CONVEY. Release the brakes only when ready to move.
