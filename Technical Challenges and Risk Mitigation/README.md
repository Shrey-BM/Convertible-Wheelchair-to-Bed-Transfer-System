# Technical Challenges and Risk Mitigation

**Convertible Wheelchair-to-Bed Transfer System**

## Problems found while designing, and how they are fixed

| Problem | Modification | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **The sliding boards were too thick:** At 41 mm thick, the boards would either press into the mattress or drop the patient by about 4 cm as they came off. | Smaller rollers and thinner boards are used. | The boards are now 28 mm thick and glide about 3 mm above the mattress. The patient will barely notice the step. |
| **Reclining the chair was too heavy:** Lowering the backrest with a patient on it needed about 214 N, which is like handling a 22 kg mass. | The gas spring is repositioned so that it helps most where the load is highest, and the grip is moved to the head end for better leverage. | Reclining now takes at most about 76 N, whether the chair is empty or holding a 120 kg patient. |
| **The crank was hard to turn at the lowest height:** At its lowest position, raising the chair needed about 139 N on the crank handle. | The crank handle is made longer, so the same effort turns the screw more easily. | The hardest point is now about 115 N, and only for the first few turns. Above 55 cm, it is under 80 N. |
| **The backrest frame was not strong enough:** With the chair flat and someone leaning on the head end, the side tubes of the backrest were working closer to their limit than is comfortable. | The tube walls are made thicker (from 2 mm to 3 mm). | The backrest frame now has a comfortable safety margin of more than 3 times its working load. |
| **The docking arm went through the bed frame and fitted only one bed:** The first docking arm simply ran into the bed frame. A later version could grip only one particular bed, so a thinner or thicker mattress, or a gap between the chair and the bed, would stop it from working. | The clamp is redesigned: an arm swings out to reach the bed, and two jaws slide up and down in steps and lock with a pin. Two hand-tightened screws finish the job. | One clamp design now fits mattresses from 9.5 to 28 cm thick, bed frames 2 to 10.5 cm deep, and a gap of up to 8 cm between the chair and the bed. |

## Potential risks while handling the chair, and their solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| **The backrest drops suddenly:** The patient falls back with the backrest. | There are two separate locks: the gas spring locks itself whenever the lever is released, and a spring pin clicks into place when the back is fully upright or fully flat. Releasing the backrest takes two separate actions. |
| **The patient rolls towards the caregiver:** The patient could fall on the far side. | The transfer bar works as a side rail, and the armrest on the caregiver's side stays on. |
| **The sliding boards rub or press on the skin:** This could cause discomfort or skin damage. | The boards glide 3 mm above the mattress. When the boards are pulled out, the belt rolls freely (in FREE mode), so it does not drag on the skin. |
| **Gaps between the seat sections under the hips or knees:** These could cause pressure or pinching under the patient. | Smooth filler strips and covers go over the gaps, and the hips rest on the seat section, not on a gap. |
| **The chair rolls away or tips during a transfer:** The patient could fall between the chair and the bed. | All four wheels lock with a pedal. The chair is clamped to the bed frame, and a hand-tightened clamp holds about 8 times more than needed. |
| **A sliding board comes out while the chair is moving:** The patient could be shifted unexpectedly. | Each board is held by a lock pin that is locked by default. To release it, pull the knob and give it a quarter turn, and it snaps back in by itself when the board slides home. |
| **The lift slowly sinks:** The chair drifts down under the patient. | The lifting screw cannot be turned back by the patient's weight. An extra friction washer and end stops add a second safeguard. |
| **The wrong belt mode is chosen:** The patient moves when they should not, or the board drags. | Push/pull handles show the mode at a glance, and checking the mode is part of the checklist. |
